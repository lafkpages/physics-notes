\noindent\textbf{Handout: Gravitational Fields -- Questions by Topic, Question 1}

Olympus Mons is the highest mountain on Mars. The height of Olympus Mons is \SI{22}{\kilo\metre}, which is 0.6\% of the radius of Mars.

The change in gravitational field strength from the bottom to the top of Olympus Mons is

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Alph*}]
  \item \( -0.6\% \)
  \item \( -1.2\% \)
  \item \( +1.2\% \)
  \item \( +0.6\% \)
\end{enumerate}

Change in gravitational field strength:

\begin{align*}
  \Delta g &= g_2 - g_1 \\
  &= \frac{GM}{r_2^2} - \frac{GM}{r_1^2} \\
  &= GM\left(\frac 1 {r_2^2} - \frac 1 {r_1^2}\right) \\
  \frac{\Delta g}{g_1} &= \Delta g \cdot \frac{r_1^2}{GM} \\
  &= r_1^2\left(\frac 1 {r_2^2} - \frac 1 {r_1^2}\right) \\
  &= \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2} - 1 \\
  &= \frac{r_1^2 - r_2^2}{r_2^2}
\end{align*}

Calculate the radii:

\begin{align*}
  r_1 \times 0.006 &= \num{22e3} \\
  r_1 &= \frac{\num{22e3}}{0.006} \\
  r_1 &= \SI{3.67e6}{\metre} \\
  r_1 + \num{22e3} = r_2 &= \SI{3.69e6}{\metre}
\end{align*}

Hence,

\begin{align*}
  \frac{\Delta g}{g_1} &= \frac{r_1^2 - r_2^2}{r_2^2} \\
  &= \frac{{(\num{3.67e6})}^2 - {(\num{3.69e6})}^2}{{(\num{3.69e6})}^2} \\
  &= -0.0109
\end{align*}
